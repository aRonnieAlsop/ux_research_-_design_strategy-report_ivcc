IV Indian Valley Website Research UX Research & Design Strategy

Indian Valley Chamber of Commerce

Designing a website around the people who actually use it.

A website research and design strategy shaped by Chamber members, administrators, community feedback and firsthand experience managing the current site.

Prepared by: Arron Alsop UX/UI Designer & Web Developer Indian Valley, California
In this report

01 — Executive Summary

This is not a redesign for appearance alone.

The purpose of this research is to understand how the current Indian Valley Chamber of Commerce website succeeds, where it creates friction and how a new website can better serve the community over the next decade.

The current site contains valuable information and has a familiar, readable visual style. It also reflects years of additions, changing administrators, separate plugins, disconnected content areas and technical workarounds.

Research shows that the strongest solution is not to make the website more elaborate. It is to make it clearer, calmer, more useful and easier to manage.

The redesign should preserve the current site's accessibility and straightforward character while reorganizing its content around two distinct user journeys: visitors exploring Indian Valley and Chamber members seeking resources.

02 — Research Methodology

Research grounded in real use—not assumptions.

Findings were gathered from conversations, direct observation and firsthand experience completing actual Chamber work.

01

02

Input from Chamber leadership regarding the current site's strengths, priorities and membership needs.

03

04

“The website should manage content—not require administrators to manage the website.”
Guiding design principle
03 — Audience Research

One website. Two very different customer journeys.

The website serves both the public and the Chamber organization, but those users arrive with different goals.

“There needs to be some kind of delineation for two different kinds of users: the people seeking membership and resources, and the tourist or potential customers.”
Chamber Business Member
Primary Audience

Visitors & Community

People exploring Indian Valley should immediately understand where they are, what is here and what they can do next.

  • Things to do
  • Events
  • Places to stay
  • Food and dining
  • Shopping and services
  • Local communities
  • Visitor information
Secondary Audience

Members & Businesses

Members need a clear, dedicated path that does not compete with visitor information.

  • Membership renewal
  • Grant opportunities
  • Business resources
  • Event participation
  • Forms and documents
  • Chamber information
  • Marketing opportunities
Research Insight

Visitors are not arriving because they are interested in Chamber administration. They are arriving because they are interested in Indian Valley.

The Chamber's role is to help them discover the community, connect with local businesses and understand what is available.

04 — Key Findings

What users told us—and what their feedback means.

Finding 01

The homepage introduces the Chamber before it introduces Indian Valley.

Users expect the homepage to explain the place first. The current welcome and Indian Valley description appear too far down the page, while time-sensitive member programs can appear more prominently.

“The welcome is way far down on the scroll. It should be first. The sign grant should be toward the end.”
Chamber Business Member

The first section should establish location, identity and opportunity. Grants and administrative notices should remain accessible within Member Resources or the Bulletin Board.

Finding 02

Navigation contains repetition and dead ends.

Some links return users to content they have already seen or fail to provide a clear next step.

“The commerce link just loops back to the members page, so either eliminate it or make it do something else.”
Chamber Business Member
“Every click I made kind of ended up in a dead end.”
Community Website User

Every page and navigation label should have a unique purpose. Every page should either complete a task or offer a clear route forward.

Finding 03

The membership directory is the most consistent area of concern.

Board members, business members and administrative users repeatedly identified membership functionality as the primary area needing improvement.

  • Members should be organized by category or industry.
  • Visitors should be able to search and filter businesses.
  • Business websites should be immediately accessible.
  • Information should come from one master record.
  • Businesses without websites should still have useful listings.
“Only some of the members have websites. I wish they all did, or that they immediately linked to their website.”
Chamber Business Member
“The main thing was all the membership stuff.”
Chamber Board Member
Finding 04

Simplicity and readability are strengths worth preserving.

Not every part of the current website needs to be replaced. Feedback indicates that its basic presentation and large typography make it approachable for older residents and users who are less comfortable with technology.

“It is pretty basic and simple, with large, easy-to-read fonts for our old folks.”
Chamber Board Member

The redesign should improve structure and functionality without becoming visually complicated or overly technical.

Finding 05

Mobile feels visually crowded.

Large sections, limited spacing and closely stacked elements can create a dense experience on a narrow screen.

“It feels claustrophobic on the phone. It is too closed up.”
Chamber Business Member

Mobile design should use generous spacing, shorter sections, comfortable margins and clear separation between ideas.

Finding 06

Motion disrupts the natural reading flow.

Entrance animations and moving content can make scrolling feel unpredictable, especially when multiple elements load at different speeds.

“I do not like the way it scrolls.”
Chamber Business Member

Motion should be limited, subtle and purposeful. Content should not make users wait for it to appear.

05 — Administrative Research

The current site is more difficult to manage than it appears.

The public sees pages. Administrators see the collection of systems, plugins, settings and workarounds required to maintain those pages.

Firsthand administrative use revealed that routine website work often required technical troubleshooting beyond what should be expected of a small Chamber administrator.

Forms and submissions

Forms initially appeared to work well, but changes to redirects caused one form to break. Another form stopped functioning after a WordPress update.

Zeffy ultimately became the easier solution because it simplified registration, submissions and payment collection without requiring the current website to manage those systems itself.

Search visibility

Google Analytics and Google Search Console had not been connected. The site's ability to communicate with Google and be properly indexed therefore required separate administrative setup.

SEO should be part of the initial build, including semantic page structure, metadata, sitemap generation, indexing controls and social-sharing information.

Firsthand Administrative Observation

A custom-built business website became easier to locate through Google search than its previous platform-based version. This reinforced the value of treating search visibility as part of the site's foundation rather than an optional feature added later.

Source: Admin/PR for IVCC

Security and access

Security protections blocked an administrator's IP address after extended website work. While the protection itself served a valid purpose, it also prevented legitimate content management.

The future system should remain secure without making normal administrative use unnecessarily difficult.

Fragmented content management

Member logos, member information, page content and image grids are edited in different areas. In some cases, administrators added business information as manually placed images rather than as structured website data.

The result feels less like a single content system and more like a series of rooms added at different times, each with its own entrance and rules.

“It feels like a Winchester Mystery House.”
Admin/PR for IVCC

HTML widgets as a workaround

HTML widgets make it possible to create custom sections inside a platform, but the custom code still lives inside the platform's theme, page builder and plugin environment.

  • Styles can conflict with the existing theme.
  • Page-builder updates can change widget behavior.
  • Responsive layouts require additional manual fixes.
  • Content may need to be entered more than once.
  • Administrators must edit code instead of completing a form.
  • Widgets do not automatically become reusable website data.
  • Custom sections remain constrained by the surrounding layout.

A personalized website can instead treat businesses, events, newsletters and notices as structured information. The administrator completes one purpose-built form, and the website determines how and where that information appears.

“Build tools for Chamber staff—not web developers.”
Administrative design principle
06 — Research to Design

Every recommendation responds to an observed problem.

Research Finding

Visitor information and member information compete on the homepage.

Design Response

Create a visitor-first homepage and a distinct Member Resources pathway.

Research Finding

Welcome and Indian Valley information are buried.

Design Response

Introduce Indian Valley, its communities and its identity at the top of the homepage.

Research Finding

Navigation repeats destinations and creates dead ends.

Design Response

Give every navigation item a unique purpose and every page a clear next action.

Research Finding

Member records are spread across multiple content areas.

Design Response

Maintain one searchable directory powered by one master business record.

Research Finding

Temporary programs dominate permanent visitor information.

Design Response

Place grants, applications and notices in Member Resources or an expiring Bulletin Board.

Research Finding

Forms and plugins can break after configuration changes or software updates.

Design Response

Use stable, purpose-built integrations and minimize plugin dependency.

Research Finding

Mobile feels crowded and animations interrupt scrolling.

Design Response

Use generous spacing, shorter content groups and minimal motion.

Research Finding

Public website comments would require ongoing moderation.

Design Response

Keep the website as the permanent record and direct conversation to email and social media.

07 — Recommended Structure

A simpler system behind a more useful public experience.

Welcome to Indian Valley

Landscape, community identity and an immediate introduction to the region.

Explore Indian Valley

Events, lodging, food, shopping, recreation and the communities that make up the valley.

Business Directory

Searchable member listings organized by category, location and service.

Events

Structured event pages with dates, locations, images, descriptions and social sharing.

Newsletters

An admin form for creating newsletters that can be emailed, archived online and shared.

Member Resources

Grants, renewals, documents, forms, applications and participation opportunities.

Community Bulletin Board

Temporary notices with expiration dates so outdated information does not remain indefinitely.

Chamber Administration

One dashboard for managing businesses, events, newsletters, documents and notices without editing website code.

08 — Design Principles

The standards that should guide every decision.

These principles translate the research into a consistent design and development philosophy.

01

Design for visitors first.

02

Build tools for Chamber staff—not web developers.

03

The website should manage content—not require administrators to manage the website.

04

Every page should help the visitor accomplish something or discover what comes next.

05

The website should be the permanent record. Social media should be the conversation.

06

Motion should support navigation, not compete with it.

07

Preserve the identity. Promote the opportunity.

08

Keep the experience open, calm and readable on every screen.

Final Direction

Keep what feels familiar. Remove what makes it difficult.

The proposed website should not abandon the current site's simplicity. It should preserve its readability while introducing clearer priorities, more useful visitor pathways and an administrative system designed specifically for the Indian Valley Chamber of Commerce.
